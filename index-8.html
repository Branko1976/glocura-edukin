A video-triggered problem-based learning (PBL) collaboration led by Lancaster Medical School and Nagoya University Graduate School of Medicine, now growing into a network across Europe, Asia and Africa.

01 · About

Learning medicine with peers from another health system

Glocura Edukin brings students from partner medical schools into one shared session. They meet the same patient on screen, write down what they noticed, and compare their reasoning in small mixed groups before each school runs the case in its own PBL tutorials.

The project rests on sociocultural learning theory: knowledge is built through social interaction, and talking with peers trained in a different setting exposes the assumptions each group brings to a clinical encounter.

Scaffold

A short clinical video

A real-time clinical scene gives every student the same starting point, regardless of language, curriculum or year.

Hook

A shared PBL case

A scenario written by Lancaster Medical School is delivered at every partner site, so the discussion carries into each local tutorial.

Dialogue

Cross-institutional groups

Breakout rooms mix students from different universities to compare impressions, priorities and ways of working.

03 · Pilot 2025

Lancaster × Nagoya: a cardiac arrest, discussed in two countries

In September 2025, students from both schools watched a hospital cardiac arrest scene from the British medical drama Casualty, wrote reflections, met in joint breakout rooms over Zoom, and then worked a Lancaster cardiovascular PBL case.

Source: pilot questionnaire, Lancaster University and Nagoya University, September 2025 (n = 12). Descriptive counts only; no inferential testing.

Seven themes in the written reflections

  1. Engagement and realism · seeing the scene made the topic stick
  2. Teamwork and communication · clear roles and open sharing
  3. Clinical and ethical decision-making · when to stop, and who decides
  4. Academic and clinical depth · some wanted more detail
  5. Educational effectiveness · easier to focus than text
  6. Structural and technical barriers · time, audio, pacing
  7. Site-dependent learning · value of the other school's view

Where the two schools put the emphasis

Lancaster

Reflections more often discussed clinical realism, medical depth and professional identity.

Nagoya

Reflections more often highlighted teamwork, communication and collective responsibility.

These are exploratory, site-level patterns from a small sample. Setting, stage of training and familiarity with a UK video may all contribute, so they are not read as national cultural traits.
